# Survey of Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning

## TL;DR
- Reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for LLM reasoning prioritize parameter-efficient fine-tuning, sample efficiency via competence-difficulty alignment, and training efficiency using selective rollouts and dynamic reward shaping to enhance reasoning capabilities under resource constraints [1][2][3][4][5].
- Applications span complex domains like mathematics, logical deduction, and multi-step problem solving, with RL facilitating exploration, self-reflection, and better reasoning path search. Some evidence exists for commonsense reasoning applications, but explicit documentation is limited in the sources [6][7][8][9][10].
- Performance evaluation relies on metrics like pass@k with verifiable or learned reward models, but suffers challenges from reward hacking, benchmark limitations, and difficulties in eliciting fundamentally new reasoning beyond base models [11][12][13][14][15].

## Background
Large language models (LLMs) have gained remarkable capabilities in natural language generation and reasoning. Reinforcement learning enhances LLMs by optimizing reasoning trajectories through trial-and-error learning with reward feedback. RL methods adapt policy optimization, reward shaping, and fine-tuning to the unique complexities of LLM reasoning, including large action spaces and multi-step logical deduction. This survey covers the core RL methods, key reasoning applications, performance evaluation frameworks, and outstanding challenges.

## Core RL Methods for LLM Reasoning
Recent RL research focuses on efficient and stable training of LLMs for reasoning under computational constraints. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as S-GRPO and T-SPMO enable improved reasoning accuracy and stability without extensive resource use [1]. Sample efficiency is enhanced with competence-difficulty alignment strategies like CDAS, which align training instances with the model's evolving skill level to optimize learning progress [3]. Selective rollout strategies (GRESO) filter uninformative prompts to reduce training costs while maintaining performance [4]. Furthermore, reward shaping techniques such as ARM dynamically balance exploration and confidence, preventing entropy collapse and improving robustness [5]. Diverse domain-specific corpora like Guru support cross-domain reasoning generalization by exposing models to varied task types [2]. Together, these methods adapt policy optimization and reward mechanisms to scale reasoning improvements effectively.

## Applications of RL in LLM Reasoning
RL is applied in diverse reasoning domains, particularly mathematics, commonsense reasoning, and multi-step logical deduction [6]. In mathematics, RL targets problem solving in algebra, geometry, and number theory with verifiable binary reward signals optimized to correct final outcomes, fostering exploration and self-reflection [7]. Logical reasoning is enhanced through training on synthetic logic puzzles with rule-based rewards that encourage exploration, verification, and backtracking [8]. Techniques combining RL with guided tree search improve multi-step reasoning by dynamically selecting efficient reasoning paths [10]. RL also supports commonsense and decision-making tasks via reinforcement learning from human feedback and preference optimization for fine-grained reasoning control [16]. These applications demonstrate RL's role in developing more robust, diverse, and interpretable reasoning patterns in LLMs.

## Evaluation and Challenges
Performance evaluation of RL for LLM reasoning mainly utilizes pass@k metrics assessing the likelihood of producing correct answers in multiple attempts, supported by automated or learned reward models [11][15]. However, fundamental challenges arise from confounding factors including RL algorithm variation, reward design, and data quality, making improvements often difficult to attribute definitively to reasoning enhancements [11]. Reward hacking, where LLMs exploit reward function flaws, leads to misaligned reasoning outputs such as repetition or flawed logic [11][14]. Benchmark vulnerabilities like data contamination and subjective reward signals complicate evaluation reliability [11][14]. Critically, studies indicate RL fine-tuning often improves sampling efficiency without significantly extending the intrinsic reasoning capabilities of base LLMs, highlighting a gap in unlocking novel reasoning capacities [12][13]. Sensitivity to hyperparameters and model specifics contributes to fragmented results, and no universal RL method dominates [13]. Open problems include designing robust, interpretable rewards, scalable training, algorithm-system co-design, and creating standardized benchmarks with minimal contamination [14]. Progress demands nuanced reward frameworks and domain-specific evaluation.

## Trends and Open Problems
Emerging trends show a move toward integrating domain-specific knowledge as in diverse corpora (Guru), and advanced reward shaping to balance exploration and exploitation (ARM) for stable reasoning training with fewer resources [2][5]. RL combined with search-based planning (e.g., policy-guided tree search) augments LLMs in multi-step reasoning [10]. The field recognizes the challenge that current RL approaches predominantly optimize existing reasoning paths rather than discover fundamentally new capabilities, prompting calls for more theory-driven and robust evaluation strategies [12][14]. There is ongoing interest in better reward modeling, including generative and rubric-based systems, to enhance learning feedback and evaluation quality [11][15]. Moreover, hyperparameter tuning, model sensitivity, and training stability remain active challenges requiring improved algorithmic solutions and co-design with system infrastructure. Overall, advancing reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning centers on bridging theory with practice to yield robust, scalable, and interpretable reasoning enhancements.
